title: "What an adapter of a space must answer was written nowhere as one contract, and the four adapters differed where nothing said they may"
---
What the domain asks of the adapter of a space was spread over three ports (ArtifactSource, MoveSource and ContentStore) and over the arms of one match in the infrastructure (the enum Adapter), and how each arm behaved was the only statement of what an adapter promises; the answers for a space with no adapter, and for an ask an adapter does not answer, sat in the infrastructure too. The four adapters (file, rust-item, rust-test, js-test) agreed, with no rule to say so, that a name leads to one thing or to an answer why it does not; that a name that matches two things gives one answer by a fixed rule (the first member of a page, twin tests joined in the order of the file); that every name asked is answered; that a text is no name by its grammar alone, before any I/O; that every hash comes from the domain; that a move of a prefix reads nothing; and that they read and write nothing else. They differed where nothing decided. A file that is there and cannot be read was an error of the space file that stopped the reading of every name, so one such file made a command give no answer and exit with 3, while the other three answered for that one name alone. The space file read a file whole with no bound of bytes, read a blob back with none, and read the whole of what Git gave of the renames since a revision, and rust-item read the cargo settings of the root with none, while their reads of the content of a name, files of tests and pages of docs, were held to 16 or 32 MiB. rust-item held a page to its docs only when it followed the link of a re-export. The hash of a content, the scan of a page of docs, the parse of what an item promises and the reading of what Git listed ran on the async threads, on inputs as large as those bounds. A space or a built-in preset registered a second time silently replaced the first.
