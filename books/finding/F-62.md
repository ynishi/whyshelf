title: "What a decision about an upstream means for what stands on it is decided again in each place that reads it, and two sentences say what no value carries"
---
A decision against a document (denied, deprecated or retired) is read from the verb of its record by Ledger::status_of, and then decided again by each reader of the declared status or of the standing made from it: frame_approve, which refuses a new approval on it; the statement of DEC-65 of whether a document counts, from two conversions of its own, one of the declared status and one of the standing; Successors::of, which says which documents count a third time; and the three arms that make the standing of freshness, a fourth. The reason an exemption is unneeded or refused passes through a bool, so which decision it was is lost. The strength of a cause (DEC-15) and its event (DEC-71) each group the same five variants of an upstream by hand, and held_back reads the standing of an upstream by a match of its own, where a decision against falls into a wildcard and what a stale or broken upstream passes on is read a second time, beside the Via of the cause that reads it in another place; that an upstream that only expired passes nothing is written in a table of a doc comment alone, and a Via holds a Severity, which can be Expired.

Three symptoms come of it. A pending upstream says only that no decision stands, and the words of the command line tell it as pending because its approval was withdrawn, a reason the value does not carry; an approval on a pending upstream, which DEC-5 allows, is told that. The table of what settles an origin says that approving again does not settle an upstream deprecated with successors because it would be repeated, when the run refuses it by DEC-5, and nothing in the value says the one is the reason of the other. And the approvals that judge approve --stale would make again (stale_taken) count one the run will refuse, because the answer of DEC-5 is in the match inside frame_approve alone and nothing before the run can read it: where such an approval also holds another event, such as an edit of its own, stale offers judge approve --stale for an origin where the run settles nothing. That happens only for a document that stands on an upstream deprecated with successors, whose approval is stale and not broken. held_back does not say a refusal and is not at fault, since a refusal is the frame (DEC-5, DEC-51). This one is not mended with the other two: the refusal is to be one function of the declared status that frame_approve and stale_taken both read, which changes what stale answers, and is the issue #176.
