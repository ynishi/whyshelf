title: "What an approval's pins come to now is decided in several places, the approval that stands is read from its record in many, and the order of the records is written by hand"
---
An approval's pins are compared with what is there now in several places, each with its own reading of now and its own rule of an edit. freshness compares the document itself (valid_causes), each upstream (pin_causes) and each artifact (artifact_causes), and says three times that two hashes that differ are a change unless a declaration joins them; pin_causes asks on its own whether the upstream is a valid document, and the artifacts read one reading of the Depends links three ways. diff compares each item of a snapshot (SnapshotItem::changed_from) by a rule of its own, with an arm for an item of one kind beside a now of the other that is never reached, and the application and the command line build again which name an artifact is read as and what it is now (ItemNow, and the now, file and changed of DiffItem). The doc of changed_from said it compares as freshness compares, which is false for an artifact whose link declares another comparison now: freshness compares no hashes there, and diff compares them the way they were pinned. judge same (frame_same_meaning) chooses on its own which pins it reads, what is there now and which declarations join an edit: the declarations of the name alone, where freshness reads those of every name of the way of a pin through its moves, so it offers again an edit declared before a move that stale tells as kept; and the pins of an approval whose document is not valid now, or that its link writes no more, which stale does not compare. This issue leaves judge same as it is: making it read the pins stale compares is a design of its own, left for issue #175. Issue #175 resolved it: judge same reads the pins stale compares, and offers exactly the edits stale tells as changes (DEC-98).

The approval that stands, and what an approval is, are read from the records in many places. Ledger::status_of and Ledger::read_pins each take the decision that stands, and the readers of a content are taken again by a walk that sorts the decisions itself. What an approval stands on, when it lapses and who made it are read from the verb of a decision by thirteen matches of their own: the declared status, the pins read now (Ledger::pins_read_now), rekey, the snapshot, the kind of a recorded approval (Asked::decision), what an approval stands on and when it lapses for the readers (two), whether a pin is read through a move, stats (twice), the names a move may be declared of, the upstreams every approval pinned, which the application lists, and whether the record judge is about to write is an approval, to say which names it pins as absent; DEC-97 says the verb is read once. Ledger::pins_read_now and Asked::decision take a decision of any ledger, and Ledger::pin_same_meaning a pin of any ledger, so an owned copy can be read against another ledger. The declared status keeps owned copies of the hashes and the pins of the approval, so a reader of the frame alone reads the pins too, and nothing ties the copy to the ledger it came from.

The order of the records is written by hand in four places (the latest decision, the walk of the readers, the cut of a reading, the order of stats), and the cut of a move is made of the parts of that key. The order of the records and the rule by which a move reaches the pins of an approval are two rules: a move reaches the pins of an approval made at or before it by time alone, an approval of the same millisecond whatever the identities, while a cut keeps records by time and then by identity. Read as one they seem to disagree; made one, a move of the same millisecond would stop reaching an approval, and freshness would change. The rule that reads a pin through moves is written in three places (Ledger::pins_read_now, the walk of the readers, and the doc of Ledger::moved_to, which called itself the one place).

A row of the answers (Row) keeps both the declared status and the decision that stands; read from one list, the two cannot part, and whether to keep both is left apart. The three ways an approval stands on what it saw, and the one vocabulary of what follows a cause, are F-56.
