The batch added crates/whyshelf-cli/src/**/*.rs to the include of the cover of cases (strength error, parts taken whole through tests, no exclude) and moved the verifications that named a file of that region whole under tests. Measured on the branch of the batch: rust-test listed 96 tests of the region in 14 files it lists whole, and 27 parts, all read: none unread; six files of the region hold no test and are no part, and need nothing. Four parts hold tests (7 in all): three were named whole by some verification already and are reached (output.rs by two, output/cause.rs, output/moves.rs); one, books/args.rs, a part for a derive of clap's Subcommand, was named by none, and is now named whole by the verification of exemptions, whose decision writes the link of a book its one test reads, with a sentence saying what it asserts. The part output.rs is also named whole by the two verifications whose sentences its tests show (the retrieval of an item with no address, a root as one word of a shell), so an edit of it makes four verifications stale. Twenty-three parts hold no test and are a gap until the person exempts them: twenty a part only for a derive of serde's Serialize, one for clap's Parser, one for serde's Deserialize and main.rs for tokio::main; none of them holds a test attribute, a module of tests or a macro called where an item goes. Before the batch one file of the region with a test was named by no verification (stale_tests.rs, 1 test); it went to the verification of stale --exit-code with a sentence. 23 verifications named files of the region; 21 of them changed, the two that named only parts did not, and three that named no file of it changed, as tests went where a sentence they show is or a sentence was added: 24 in all. The 96 tests are 98 names under cases: 2 tests evidence a sentence in each of two verifications. No test fits the text of no verification, so none is a candidate for an exemption. Tests went by evidence: the seven tests of new_tests.rs that read the lines of new --from went to the verification of new --from, whose sentences were said of the binary, with a sentence of what the command line reads, and two tests of comparison_tests.rs, which no sentence of the two that named the file says, went to the verification of diff and of the links to artifacts. Fifteen verifications changed their text, in sixteen places, each adding what a named test asserts that the text did not; no sentence was found that a test asserts otherwise, so none was set right. A blind review that read every named test's assertions found eight points, each checked against the code and set right: a sentence said --standing may be given more than once where its test gives it once, one said the commands after init parse as the commands meant where its test asserts only that they parse and how the last reads, one gave the record of a declaration about a file fields its test asserts only of a document, and one said a link of a book is read as --from and cover --link write it where its test reads the text alone, and these four were cut to what the tests assert; three tests evidenced no sentence of their verification (the kind of a slug key in the answer of map, the warning line of a warn gap, an approved content that is not UTF-8), and a sentence saying what each asserts was added; and the test of the commands after init moved to the verification of init, whose decision gives them, with a sentence. So 25 verifications changed, 17 of them in their text. After the batch check lists only the twenty-three parts that await an exemption beside the exemptions already recorded, fmt --check is clean, stale lists exactly the 25 verifications changed, each with links_changed and the seventeen whose text changed also self_changed, and the test of this repository's verifications passes. The guard's tests took 8.42 s and 5.03 s on two threads; its candidates built in 5 min 54 s from a cold build. The full fmt check, clippy, docs and tests took 18 min 25.72 s from a cold build, over five minutes: the builds took 15 min 40 s of it (clippy 4 min 34 s, docs 58.45 s and 1 min 57 s, tests 8 min 11 s); the tests alone, built, took 1 min 38.51 s, the slowest binaries 18.91 s, the unit tests of whyshelf-infra 18.55 s and 8.43 s. The time the split took and the asks of a listing were not measured.
