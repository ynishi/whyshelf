A rule of who may decide (DEC-88) may ask for approvals, from 1, the default, to 10: how many different names approve the same content before an approval of its book settles, and how many declare an edit before it keeps the meaning. The need of a record is read in one place, beside its admission, from the rule that reads it with its book: that rule's approvals, admitted or open; 1 when no rule reads it (an unruled book, a record made before since, a project that declares no rule). Whether an approval has its names is one judgement of the ledger, the quorum of the approval that stands (StandingApproval::quorum): the readers of the content that stands (DEC-58; an upstream renumbered since is the same content, so the names given before still count), each name once however many of its approvals are among them, counting while its own latest reading has not lapsed by the one rule of expiry, whoever approved last, so an approval waits again once any reading it needed lapses, in either order; only when every reading lapsed is the reader of the approval that stands counted alone (the approval is expired, which freshness tells), so a rule of one name is never short. The quorum is met or short, a fact kept apart from freshness: Freshness and Severity are unchanged, and the level shown, Standing::kind, is awaiting for a fresh approval short of names and the level of its freshness for any other, a degraded one short of names listing its quorum beside it. stale and stale --exit-code read degraded approvals only, so an awaiting document fails neither, and --stale does not take it: another name settles it, by naming it; check and status list every short quorum, and check stays clean. An upstream whose approval is short, at any freshness, an expired one included, does not stand as a settled approval: what stands on it is stale through it as on a pending upstream (UpstreamDeclared of Unseconded), a run of --stale or --pending holds it back as awaiting, read before the expiry that would let it through, and stale offers judge approve of the upstream with --actor and a name to write in, since the same name again would only repeat its reading. A declaration of the same meaning is used for a way only once each edge, one pair of hashes, counts: for some declaration of the edge, the different names that declared it are at least as many as that declaration needs (the rule of the record's book, and [decide] for an artifact), so the least need of its declarations, and a declaration added, such as one made after a rule's since beside one made before it, never takes back an edge that counted; names are counted per edge and never per way, over the declarations freshness reads for the pin (of every name of an artifact's way through its moves). check lists as same_meaning_short an edge with fewer names that a pin that stale reads now meets (one of its ends is the hash pinned, or is joined to it by edges that count), and stays clean; an edge no such pin meets is not told. A name that declared an edge declares it again to no end, as one name counts once, along the moves of every pin that holds the hash, as stale reads each pin: judge same refuses it (exit 2, writing nothing) saying that name already declared the edit and it awaits another name, as judge approve leaves out a repeat. A rule whose names that may approve, whatever their times, are fewer than its approvals is a problem of the declaration, and so is a [decide] that asks for fewer than a book or reads fewer records than a book that asks for more than one name (its since later than the book's, or set where the book has none), since a declaration of the same meaning of an artifact keeps the approvals of every book that pins it, and one made before [decide]'s since would be ruled by none and need one name. A name is as declared (DEC-34): one person who writes two names is two names. Known limitation: after the first of N approvals, what stands on the document is stale until the Nth, so a CI that runs stale --exit-code (DEC-26) fails meanwhile. A project that declares no rule needs one name everywhere, reads and writes as before, and counts nothing: its quorum is met without reading the readers, and a declaration of the same meaning is an edge of its own.
