The adapter of the space rust-item reads, from the page it finds an item at, what the item promises, and gives it as one text. The lookup hands over where a name is (a place: a module, an item with its kind, or a member with its kind and the anchor of its element, each with its page); the one reader of a page, promise_of, makes of it a Promise: Declared, a declaration with docs, for a kind that has a declaration, and DocsOnly for a module, a crate, a primitive type and a keyword (one table of kinds says which; attribute and derive macros have a declaration on their pages, as #[name]). Docs that are not there are Docs::Absent, a promise too, so docs written or taken away change the text. A page that has no declaration where its kind has one, one not laid out as read, and one that cannot be read or is too large are Unreadable, with why. The declaration of an item is its item-decl and its docs are its top doc; the declaration of a member is its code header (the code of a field) and its docs the docblock right after its element, inside the same toggle for a method and after the mark of a deprecated item; a variant has the list of its fields after that. The list of impls, the other members, the line Source and the sidebar are not read; but the declaration of a type is what rustdoc writes, which lists the public fields of a struct, the variants of an enum and the signatures of the items of a trait, and the declaration of a constant holds its value, so those are part of the promise of that item, and a member named on its own is its own promise. The tags are taken out, the entities (numeric too) put back, and a block tag or a line break ends a line. The text is each line of the declaration after decl and each line of the docs after doc, an empty line empty, the declaration first, and white space is not collapsed: Promise::text is the one place that forms it. resolve gives the reading of the text of the promise (ArtifactReading::of_bytes), so each comparison asked hashes it, lines with white space collapsed and blank lines left out and bytes as they are; an Unreadable promise gives ArtifactReading::there with its reason, so presence is still told there and any other comparison cannot be told. current gives the same text, or none when it cannot be told, for a link of any comparison, so the diff report item of the library carries it; the diff command shows none, as it shows the current text only when the approved one can be read back and nothing keeps it at approval (the revisit below). The adapter offers the preset rustdoc-promise: space rust-item, compare lines, means depends, beside rustdoc, which stays as it was; a link that changes from one to the other is a change of comparison. The limits (a layout rustdoc may change, members of one name told by the first, the docs of a method of a trait impl being the trait's, the impls of a type left out of its promise, the mark of a deprecated item left out) are written on the module of the adapter.
