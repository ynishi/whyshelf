title: "The space rust-item suggests where a gone name moved from the names its built docs hold now, and a move of a prefix is suggested only when it stands against what is there now"
---
The adapter of rust-item answers suggestions from the names its built docs hold now: a module is a directory with an index page and an item a page of one of the kinds of items, in the crates of the project that carry the mark of rustdoc (the docs of a dependency are not walked, and docs not of their sources are not searched, DEC-116), the gone names' crates walked first and the others in order of name, with no anchor of a re-export, no redirect and no path that leads outside the root and the build directory; every name it lists is one its reading says is there. A candidate of a gone name is a name of the docs whose last k segments are the gone name's and whose leading part differs, k the largest found in each crate, so that a name of another crate that ends more alike hides none of the gone name's crate; a member Type::m is a candidate of a member only when the page of its owner's candidate has a member of that name, as a lookup of the member reads one, the owners without it left out before the largest k is taken, and the names that end as the whole member does (a function m) are candidates beside them, at the largest k of their crate. The bounds of pages and bytes are shared by every gone name, searched one after another; within one, the promises of the candidates in its crate are read before the others, and a page read already, such as the page of a member's type, is used whatever the bound, so that a bound spent by the names searched before can leave a later name's candidate found by its end only, with the note that the search stopped. When the pin holds content, the candidate's promise is hashed under the pin's comparison: the same content is same_content, otherwise same_end, a way of being found of its own (FoundBy::SameEnd) that is neither the content nor a history. Each name keeps at most 16 candidates, those in the gone name's crate kept before others of the same rank, and says when it cut them; the answer orders them as MoveCandidate does (same_content, then renamed by similarity, then same_end), then by name. Each candidate carries every move of a prefix that leads to it: every pair of a leading part or the whole of each name under which the gone name becomes the candidate by the same rule judge moved --prefix reads (DEC-80), the widest first and the move of the whole names last; the boundary of a name stays the adapter's, so DEC-80 keeps its rule and the domain only counts the moves as equal values. The walk is held to 20,000 names, a depth of 64, 2,000 pages and 512 MiB, and a page to its bound (DEC-84); past a bound, an unreadable directory or page, a page too large and a promise that cannot be hashed are notes of the name, and docs whose place is not known are a note that the search is unavailable, never an error. The core then tries the moves of a prefix that two or more gone names carry among the candidates kept (a name whose candidate was cut counts toward none of its moves), those carried by the candidates found best first (in the order of MoveCandidate), then those that most names carry, up to 32, the rest counted: each goes through the steps of judge moved --prefix, the move read by the adapters, planned, the old and new names read once for all of them, and framed (DEC-80); whether the actor is admitted is not tried and no record is accepted. One that stands is offered with the names it moves and the spaces it does not ask, one refused with how many it cannot move; the offers are alternatives, each tried alone against what is there now, so that once one is declared another that moves the same names has nothing under it. moves declares nothing (DEC-60): the candidates and the prefixes are suggestions, and the person declares with judge moved. A rename in place (m::Foo to m::Bar) has no candidate, and a name reachable only by a public re-export is none.
