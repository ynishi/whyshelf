whyshelf keeps what a person approved, as it was given, the way a commit keeps what was committed, and answers whether it is still what is there; it never judges whether it is right. Of DEC-25 this keeps that whyshelf does not judge whether a statement is true, which is a person's review, named by the approval's reason. Of REQ-2 it keeps that whether an approval holds is derived on every read and never stored, and that a record is read back as it was written. Of DEC-84 it keeps that an adapter answers what a content is, never whether it is right, and the contract a space and its adapter meet, in which each ask an adapter may answer beside the three every adapter answers is declared by the type of the adapter. Of REQ-36 it keeps that how fine the thing a name leads to is, and the form of a name, are the space's own.

A new kind of fact is put in exactly one of five places before it is designed, and the requirements and decisions of that place are read first, their rejected forms and revisit included; where the fact contradicts one, that decision is changed first.

- What a person saw when approving: a pin of the approval, compared with what is there on every read. It is pinned in three ways (the document itself, the upstream documents, the artifacts, F-56); each way has its own pin and reading, what a pin comes to now is decided once for each kind of content, a document's or an artifact's (DEC-98), each way tells it in causes of its own, and what follows its causes is the one vocabulary of events, strengths and settlements.
- A person's declaration: a record of the ledger (a decision, a revocation, a declaration that an edit keeps the meaning, a move, an exemption), with its actor, its time and its reason. A revocation names the ids of the decisions and declarations it withdraws; every other record is matched to what it is about by the document or the artifact it names and, where it names one, by its content.
- A name: the key of a document and the name of an artifact are resolved when the shelf is built; the reading of a pin through the moves declared since it was made is derived from the ledger on every read, never stored.
- What a link can name, and how fine the thing is: a space and its adapter, which meets the contract of DEC-84 (REQ-36); the domain does not know the form of its names.
- What the project declares of its books: the books and their fields, and for each link what it names, how many, what it means and whether every document it can name must be reached by one, and for a link to artifacts what of a thing it compares and its preset, with the defaults of the declaration (DEC-10, DEC-53); each pin keeps the comparison its hash was made with.

A program's verdict on the content, such as whether the tests pass or whether CI succeeded, is kept in none of them: it would be a second judgement beside the approval. Content an adapter reads is kept as any content is, even when a program wrote it: the docs that cargo doc builds, from which rust-item reads what an item promises for the preset rustdoc-promise to compare (DEC-83).

Each issue starts from what it should make possible, written apart from the means its text proposes, and its design chooses the means by that.
