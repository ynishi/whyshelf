title: "The adapter of a space answers three asks every adapter answers and three it may, keeps the promises of identifying, reproducibility and content only, and one conformance test holds every adapter to them"
---
Every adapter of a space answers three asks. It says its space and its built-in presets, each preset for its own space (DEC-53). It reads names: every name asked has one of the four answers of an ArtifactReading (DEC-45), its hashes made by ArtifactReading::of_bytes from the bytes it read (DEC-10). It gives the current bytes of a name: the bytes the reading hashed under bytes, or none. It may answer three more, each declared by the type of the adapter, so that the compiler holds the declaration to the implementation: the move of a prefix of its names (DEC-80), suggestions of where a name that is gone moved (DEC-60), and the content at an address it gave (DEC-7); an adapter that gives an address reads back by it what its store holds, and an address of a content its store does not hold (a file Git never added) reads back none. An adapter that does not answer one names the not-answering type of the core, and the core gives the fixed answer without calling it. The core dispatches each ask to the adapter that names its space and holds the answers for a space with no adapter: those of the not-answering types, cannot tell for each name read, and none for the current bytes. It reaches an adapter through two visits, one with no I/O (its space and presets, the move of a prefix) and one that reads (a reading, the current bytes, suggestions, reading back), so that the infrastructure keeps only the list of the adapters and the two matches that hand a visit to the adapter at a number, and holds no rule. Two adapters that collide are refused (DEC-86). The three ports the use cases see keep their shape: the dispatch answers ArtifactSource and MoveSource, and reads an artifact back for ContentStore. The promises: identifying, a name leads by the rule of its space to one thing, or to an answer that it does not, and a name that can match two things gets one answer by a fixed rule; reproducible, the same name over the same content gives the same kind, hash and reason, however often, in whatever order and with whatever else it is asked, and the content does not depend on where the tree sits, the time or the locale (the address and the suggestions are outside this); content only, an adapter never answers whether the content is right (DEC-25). The rules: a text is no name by the grammar alone, with no I/O; what is not certain cannot be told; one name that cannot be read cannot be told (DEC-85), but for an item of rust-item whose own page cannot be read, which is there under presence and cannot be told under a comparison of its content (DEC-54); every adapter holds each thing it reads to a bound of bytes (a file, a page, a file of settings, and of what Git gives, its listing of the work tree, its answer of the renames and a blob read back), one that walks nesting holds it to a depth, and one that runs a program holds each run to a time; past the bound of a name, that name cannot be told (or, for such an item of rust-item, is there under presence only), and a run of a program past its time while names are read is an error of the whole ask, with no answer, while in suggestions Git's renames past their time or their bound are a note of the name (DEC-60); nothing is cut or passed over to answer another thing; it reads and writes nothing else, and reads nothing outside the root or the declared build directory (DEC-54); and what of these may be larger than 1 MiB (a content, a page, a file of tests, Git's listing of the work tree and its answer of the renames) is parsed, scanned and hashed on a blocking thread when it is, not on the async ones. The bounds are the adapters': a file of the space file 16 MiB, and as much of a blob read back, Git's answer of the renames since a revision 16 MiB, a page of docs 32 MiB, the cargo settings 1 MiB, a file of tests 16 MiB. The names stay opaque to the domain (DEC-44, DEC-80): the domain fixes the shape of the answers and how content is hashed, and reproducibility is a rule of the contract, checked by the conformance test and not when the shelf is read. Each adapter writes on its module page, under seven fixed headings, the grammar of its names, their positions, equal names, the one thing of a name, its answers, what is compared and where it reads; the domain does not read them. One conformance test takes an adapter and its test hook, asks the adapter only as a project asks it, through the dispatch over a registry of that adapter alone, so that the move of a prefix and reading back are answered the way its type declares, and the hook writes a tree, says what each name of it should come to, and declares the bounds of bytes of the adapter and how it tells a thing that cannot be read. It checks the space the adapter names and its presets, that every name is answered with no error, the answer of each name, that a text is no name whatever is there, current, the same answers asked again, alone and in halves and over the tree in another place, reading back, a name of two things, two names of one thing, the move of a prefix, one name or root past each bound of bytes declared and none other, a name past the depth for an adapter that walks nesting, and the seven headings. It does not check the blocking threads, that nothing else is read or written, the bound of time (the tests of the space file hold it on their own), or the time and the locale. A kind of name the hook cannot make on the machine it runs on is printed to standard error as not checked, never passed.
