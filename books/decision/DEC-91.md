title: "A record keeps ran_by beside its actor only when another ran the command, given by --ran-by or WHYSHELF_RAN_BY and never by Git or a file, and no judgement reads it"
---
A record of any kind (a decision of any verb, a revocation, a declaration of the same meaning, of a move or of an exemption) may carry an optional key ran_by, a name in the form of an actor, beside its actor: who ran the command, when it is not the one who answers for the record. The actor stays as it was (DEC-34) and is the only name any rule reads. The key is written only when ran_by differs from the actor, and the record stays version 1, as the other keys added after the first form do: a record without it is written and read in the form, and with the hash, it always had (DEC-2). A name that is the actor's is one name wherever it comes from: a ran_by given equal to the actor is dropped when the record is made, and a stored record whose ran_by is its actor is not read (RecordProblem::RanByIsActor), never read as one name, since the record read back would not be the record stored (DEC-46). A stored ran_by with spaces around it is read trimmed, as a stored actor always has been. Two records of one id that differ only in ran_by conflict, as any two different records do (DEC-13). The two names are compared where an Attribution is made, and nowhere else. ran_by is given by --ran-by on every judge verb, else by the variable WHYSHELF_RAN_BY (blank or not text counts as not set), else there is none; Git's user.name and the files of the project are never asked, so, as for the actor, nothing committed chooses it. It is asked of the actor port with the actor, once per run, where a use case settles who it writes as (AttributionAsk, ActorSource). ran_by is told and read by no judgement: not who may decide, not who has read a document (and so not what an approval repeats or is a second reading of), not the declarations an actor made, and not the runs stats counts (DEC-55), which stay a time and an actor. The answers that show a record's actor (the decision of a row, get --approved, the exemptions check lists) show ran_by beside it, null when there is none; the version of the answers is unchanged. A build from before this does not read a record that has ran_by, and its check fails on it; its other commands act as if the record were not there, so its judge can write over that decision with no warning; every other record is the same for both. Known limitation: whyshelf cannot tell an environment variable that a committed file set (a direnv .envrc, say) from one a person set, so such a file can name who ran the command, as it can the actor.
