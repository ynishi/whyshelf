What is foreseen answers, for a document, that the frame takes an approval of it, that it refuses it and why, or that it was not asked; it no longer answers yes or no. settlements, still the one place that says what settles an origin, lists the candidates in order once and then puts each approval by name where that answer says: among what can settle the origin when the frame takes it, among the approvals withheld, with the frame's refusal, when the frame refuses it, and nowhere when the frame was not asked. An origin is so settled with two lists (Settled): what can be said or done, as before, and the approvals the frame refuses, each with why (Withheld). An approval by name is one type (Approval: of a pending upstream, by another name, again with an expiry), which a settlement carries, so the three are written once and an approval withheld cannot be anything else.

stale tells each approval withheld at its origin, in withheld: the document, which approval, and the sentence of the frame's refusal, the one judge approve --stale gives for a document it leaves out as refused; and writes one line for each on stderr, with the kind of approval said, so two approvals of one document are two lines. It is told, not offered: no command is written for it, so next is as it was, and it is told whatever the root of the run. It is told at every origin it keeps an approval from, also when the refusal can be read elsewhere in the answer. The kind of the refusal and what it names are in the sentence only, as for what judge approve leaves out.
