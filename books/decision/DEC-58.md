A new approval is told apart, once, in the ledger beside the frame, by what it stands on and who approves (ApprovalKind). Its readers are the actors of the approvals of the same content that stand together now: walking back from the latest decision about the document that is not revoked, the approvals that stand on what it stands on, until a decision that does not. Freshness reads the approval that stands, the latest, with its expiry and its artifact pins through the moves declared since it. A new approval of that content that would approve it again once it lapsed, make the document lapse at another time, or end a move's reading of a pin, renews it, whoever makes it. Of the others, which change nothing freshness reads, one by an actor with no reading among them is a second reading; one by a reader whose latest reading has the expiry asked repeats, since it would change nothing at all; one by a reader otherwise renews that reading, which the reader keeps whoever's approval stands, so a rule-chosen run writes it as it writes any renewal; of other content, an approval is new. What an approval stands on is every document pin by link, identity, key and hash, every artifact pin by link, space and name, comparison, and present with its hash or absent, and the document's own hash; addresses, the revision and the reason are not compared, and freshness is not asked: an approval stale only through an upstream, asked with its expiry, repeats for a reader whose reading has it and is a second reading for another actor, and one fresh only by a declared same meaning has a new hash and does not. judge approve writes no record for an approval that repeats, lists it under left_out with the reason repeats and the approval that stands, warns of it on stderr, and judges and writes the rest of the run; --again writes it anyway. It writes the others, a second reading included, in every project, whether or not it declares a rule. A run first checks what it is asked (an expiry not after now, a reference that names nothing, each refused as before), then asks for the actor once, before any of this is decided; with no name the run fails and writes nothing (DEC-34). A run where every document is left out writes nothing and exits 0. approve --stale writes no second reading: a document it takes whose approval would be one is left out as second_reading with the approval that stands, and one that would repeat as repeats, while an expired one is renewed by anyone's run; neither holds back what stands on it (a pending document, which --pending takes, has no approval to read again). A second reading is said by naming the document. The other verbs decide as before, but ask for the actor at the same point, so with no name one that would be refused fails on the name first.
